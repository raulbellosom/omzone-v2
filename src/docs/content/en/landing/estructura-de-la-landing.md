# How the public site is structured

The **public site** is what everyone sees before they log in or create an account. It's your storefront — the first impression for every potential client.

---

## Main pages

### Home

Your brand's first impression. The home page presents who you are, what you offer, and why it matters.

It typically includes:
- A hero section with a striking image or video and a clear call to action.
- A curated selection of featured experiences.
- Editorial sections: benefits, testimonials, moments gallery, your philosophy.
- A footer with contact info, social links, and legal pages.

---

### Experience catalog

A browsable listing of all active experiences. Visitors can filter by type and explore what's available.

---

### Experience page

When a visitor clicks on an experience, they land on its detail page. This is where they read the full story, see photos, review pricing, and start the booking process.

→ See **Experience page** for a full breakdown.

---

### Additional pages

Depending on your setup, the site may also include:
- **About** — Your story and philosophy.
- **Contact** — A form for general inquiries.
- **Blog / Articles** — Editorial content published as standalone articles.
- Legal pages — Privacy policy, terms and conditions.

---

## The navigation bar

At the top of every page:
- Your logo.
- Main navigation links.
- A **Log in** / **Sign up** button.

---

## The footer

At the bottom:
- Contact information.
- Social media links.
- Quick links to legal pages.

---

## What drives the public site content

The site pulls from two sources:

1. **Publications** — All the text, photos, and editorial narrative you create in the admin panel.
2. **Active experiences** — Any experience with **Active** status appears in the catalog.

> If an experience isn't showing up on the site, check that it's set to **Active** and has an active publication linked to it.
